# DinoMath 0.4 — Curriculum design and teaching roadmap

## The issue this revision addresses

The first three versions presented four destinations, but sessions still concentrated on one narrowly defined skill. DinoMath 0.4 gives each of eight fictional locations a mathematical purpose, with:

1. **A visual concept workshop** using an appropriate representation (e.g. fraction bar, number line, balancing equation).
2. **A Discovery Trail** that can interleave an accessible prerequisite, focus skill, related skill and review.
3. **Optional Skill Focus** to provide the short, structured practice that makes Kumon's mastery structure useful.

These modes are deliberately complementary. Neither age nor school year is shown in the child's interface. A parent can directly select a micro-skill at any stage.

## Progression

The 65-skill curriculum is unchanged in coverage (32 currently playable). It spans KS1 arithmetic foundations, KS2 number, multiplication, fractions, decimals and geometry, and early KS3 directed numbers, ratio and equations. A skill has four successive tiers. Individual tier progression is based on completed attempts and the last ten **independent** answers. Mixed trails credit the specific skill answered, not the trail's target skill.

**Current default route recipe**, cycle of five puzzle roles:

| Position | Role | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warm-up | Revisit a prerequisite or familiar skill |
| 2 | Main challenge | Practise current target |
| 3 | Explore a connection | Contrast with another skill in the same location |
| 4 | Main challenge | Second focus problem, often a different format |
| 5 | Recall | Revisit a due error if one is available; otherwise focus again |

The app allows sessions shorter than five; there are no artificial penalties for stopping. The role labels are visible so children are not surprised by the switch. This is a **simple rule-based route**, not a learning-science-validated recommendation engine. It does not yet ensure a long-term retention check after each skill is mastered.

## Better teaching support

For representative skills, Dr Helix now provides a *conceptual explanation* and offers a no-score manipulative workshop. The workshop operates independently of the question and does not grant mathematical mastery. It supports the concrete/pictorial/abstract connection where it is appropriate, rather than using dinosaur pictures as decoration on every equation.

The next version should add carefully identified **misconception tags** tied to actual distractors and errors. For example, when a learner answers `1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5`, recognise the characteristic *adding denominators* misunderstanding, show an equal-parts model, and try a simpler diagnostic task before returning to a structurally different version.

## Known curriculum gaps

**KS1:** money, time, symmetry and early data diagrams are in the roadmap but not playable.

**KS2:** column methods with exchange, long division, factors/primes representation, mixed numbers, fraction multiplication, decimal operations and 2D/3D geometry require development or stronger manipulative support.

**KS3:** inequalities, algebraic expression construction, graphs, indices, sequences and geometric reasoning remain planned. Some current tiers differ mostly in number size rather than conceptual complexity.

**Accessibility:** workshop manipulatives are clickable and have textual descriptions, but full screen-reader and keyboard-only usability has not been audited. Multiple representations should be tested with children who prefer quiet mode, including children for whom large animated feedback is uncomfortable.

**Personalisation:** offer optional diagnostic placement; check independent retention several days later; avoid automatically increasing difficulty after every run of success; distinguish miscalculation from concept misunderstanding; and learn from a child's *reported* experience as well as accuracy.

## Design evaluation

With Adam and other volunteers (with appropriate family consent), record qualitative observations rather than only scores:

- Does the child choose to return without prompting?
- Are question switches predictable and welcomed, or do they create anxiety?
- Does opening the workshop help after an error?
- Are the wrong-answer explanations useful and short enough?
- Does the next question appropriately revisit the *idea* without repeating exactly the same numbers?
- Are the routes pitched at the right challenge level for uneven learning profiles?

**The first success criterion is positive engagement and a reduction in frustration**, not completing as many calculations as possible.


## V4.2 Fossil museum maths extension

- Reconstruct four simplified, species-labelled fossil mounts by matching seven anatomical sections (informal spatial reasoning and scientific vocabulary; not counted as maths mastery).
- Optional proportional reasoning model: change a hypothetical museum model length and scale factor and watch equal groups accumulate. The numbers are illustrative, not authentic dinosaur lengths.
- Future: add user-selectable measurement puzzles, shape symmetry and an evidence-based fossil reconstruction task; keep these optional and separate from assessed practice.
